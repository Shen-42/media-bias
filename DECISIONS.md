# Decisions

Judgment calls made in autonomous mode. Each entry gives the date, the decision, the alternatives, and the reason. Codebook entries (D-01 to D-17) are referenced from `codebook.md`.

## Codebook (2026-11-19)

| ID | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | In scope: a motor vehicle (including motorcycles, buses, streetcars) struck a pedestrian or cyclist. A cyclist striking a pedestrian is out. Wheelchair and mobility-device users count as pedestrians; e-bike and e-scooter riders count as cyclists; motorcyclists are not VRUs. | Include cyclist–pedestrian crashes; treat micromobility as its own group | Q10 names motor vehicles as striking vehicles. Ontario law treats mobility-device users as pedestrians. Too few micromobility crashes for a separate group. |
| D-02 | Impact verbs extended to: hit, strike, run over, collide, crash (into), knock down, mow down, pin, drag, clip, door, back into, plow into, slam into, ram. | Keep the four-verb list | Four verbs miss common crash wording; the filter would drop real crash sentences. |
| D-03 | "accident" is a collision noun. | Score `accident_term` on all sentences | Without it, "The accident happened at…" never reaches `accident_term`, which would undercount the label the guidelines care most about. |
| D-04 | "A cyclist died" alone is not a crash sentence; "died after the crash" is. | Add death verbs to the filter | Keeps the filter tied to the collision itself, as defined in Q11. |
| D-05 | Impact verb present → label by its form; no impact verb but a collision noun → `nominal`; `intransitive` = impact verb with no struck road user as object (incl. "X and Y collided"). First impact clause wins. | `intransitive` for any intransitive main verb (e.g. "died") | Removes the overlap between `intransitive` and `nominal` and makes the label computable from the parse. |
| D-06 | `driver_reference = vehicle` when a vehicle appears in the impact clause as agent or other party; "the TTC" counts as vehicle. | Vehicle only as agent | "Collided with a car" still describes the crash through the vehicle, not the driver, which is the framing the label measures. |
| D-07 | `focus` for a conjoined subject = first conjunct. | New value `mixed` | Keeps the codebook's four values; first conjunct is what a reader sees as the subject. |
| D-08 | "A cyclist and a car collided" → `vru_as_actor = yes`. "Stepped into the path" → `vru_conduct_detail`, not actor. | The reverse | The sentence presents the VRU as a co-agent; movement without an impact verb is conduct, not grammatical agency. |
| D-09 | `accident_term`: lemma "accident" only; not "accidentally", "incident", "mishap". | Wider softener lexicon | Matches the Road Collision Reporting Guidelines focus and prior studies' "accident" counts. |
| D-10 | `vru_conduct_detail` is scored on every VRU-mentioning sentence in a crash article (`vru_sentence`), counts details in either direction, and rolls up to `vru_conduct_any`. | Crash sentences only; fault-implying details only | Conduct details usually sit in their own sentence; restricting to crash sentences would miss most of them. Counting both directions avoids judging fault. |
| D-11 | `text_source = quoted` only for direct speech whose impact clause (or most of the sentence) is inside quotation marks. Indirect speech goes through release matching; a boolean `attributed` records attribution. | Indirect speech counts as quoted | Otherwise most crash sentences ("police say…") would be `quoted` and the police-matching comparison would be empty. |
| D-12 | `driver_exceptional`: fled, impaired, Criminal Code charge, street racing/stunt driving. HTA charges recorded separately (`driver_hta_charge`), not exceptional. Speeding or suspended licence alone: not exceptional. | Any charge counts | HTA tickets are routine in Toronto releases; counting them would make ordinary cases "exceptional" and blur the Fevyer & Aldred "rogue driver" split. |
| D-13 | `thematic_frame` from sentence-level `thematic_cue`; a speaker counts only when referring to a cause, pattern, or fix beyond this crash. | Count any politician or advocate quote | Condolences are episodic; the label is about systemic framing. |
| D-14 | `police_overlap` denominator = non-quoted body sentences; null without a linked release. | All sentences; zero without a release | Quotes cannot be police-matched by definition (precedence); null avoids mixing "no release" with "no overlap". |
| D-15 | `severity` stored for both article and crash; crash keeps the worst outcome. | One of the two | Follow-ups often report a death later; both views are needed. |
| D-16 | `road_users`: transit vehicle adds `driver` only if an operator is mentioned. | Always add `driver` | Matches the text, not an inference. |
| D-17 | `article_type = initial` per outlet; label by fetched content; store fetch time. | First article across all outlets | Per-outlet comparisons need each outlet's own first report. |
| D-18 | Codebook examples stay synthetic. | Replace with real Toronto sentences | Keeps article text out of the repo and out of Qwen prompts, and removes any chance of test leakage. `eval/excluded_ids.txt` still exists and is enforced. |

## Collection and rules (2026-11-19)

| ID | Decision | Alternatives | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-29 | Quote detection uses spaCy + regex; the Gender Gap Tracker extractor is not used. | Port the GGT code | GGT targets speaker attribution with stale dependencies; v1 only needs direct-quote spans and an attribution flag. Simpler and fully tested. |
| D-30 | Bike lane and transit URLs are discovered and stored but not fetched in v1. | Fetch their raw text in the same crawl | The author allowed but did not require it; fetching ~1,200 extra CityNews pages at 10 s each would add hours with no v1 use. |
| D-31 | "hit-and-run" counts as a collision noun for the crash-sentence filter. | Leave it out | "Police are investigating a hit-and-run involving a cyclist" describes the collision; excluding it would drop a common crash-sentence form. |
| D-32 | Fetch order: URLs whose slug names a VRU or a strike verb first; slugs matched only by "crash"/"collision" after, as time allows. | Fetch in date order | CityNews requires a 10 s delay; 4,700 candidates would take 13 h. Most crash-only slugs are car-only or highway crashes, which are out of scope. Tier-2 coverage is reported. |
| D-33 | CBC excluded: robots.txt unreachable from this network on three attempts (2026-11-18, 2026-11-19 twice), treated as "do not crawl". Retried again 2026-11-19 15:20 CST: connection refused before any HTTP response. | Crawl without robots.txt | CLAUDE.md requires respecting robots.txt; an unreadable robots.txt gives no permission. |
| D-34 | Wire-copy dedupe: MinHash over 5-word shingles, Jaccard ≥ 0.7; earliest article is canonical. | Embedding similarity; lower threshold | Wire copy is near-verbatim; 0.7 tolerates outlet edits (added lines, trimmed endings) without merging different stories about the same crash. |
| D-35 | SetFit trains on confident Snorkel labels only (P ≥ 0.7 or ≤ 0.3), at most 200 positives and a matched number of negatives. | All rows with soft labels | SetFit is a few-shot method; confident, balanced examples suit it and keep training fast on CPU/MPS. |
| D-36 | Qwen labels 8 sentences per call; it runs only on the triage pools (1,500 crash, 3,000 context sentences) and the eval rows. | One sentence per call; whole corpus | qwen3:4b on an 8 GB M2 is slow; batching shares the long system prompt. Qwen is one LF among several, so it does not need full coverage. |
| D-37 | Eval sets: crash (100 test: 60 random + 40 disagreement; 30 dev), context (same sizes, for the two fuzzy labels), text_source (40 dev for tuning, 40 test). | Crash sentences only | Fuzzy labels and text_source need their own units (D-26); text_source needs the release text shown to the annotator. |
| D-38 | Slugs matched only by "-hit-" or "struck" without a VRU or strike construction are skipped as noise. | Fetch all | They were mostly wire stories ("prices hit record high", "bill struck down"); each CityNews fetch costs 10 s. |
| D-39 | A person struck by a vehicle ("a woman in her 70s was hit by a streetcar") counts as a pedestrian when no occupant cue (driver of, passenger, his car) appears. | Require the word "pedestrian" | Many reports never say "pedestrian"; requiring it dropped clear pedestrian crashes. |
| D-40 | CityNews Toronto and CTV Toronto articles are treated as Toronto unless another police service or GTA city is named; Global articles must mention Toronto. | Require "Toronto" everywhere | Local-section articles often omit the city name. |
| D-41 | Passive detection checked on UD EWT test (gold: Voice=Pass or a :pass dependent on VBN tokens). Added one general fix: a bare VBN conjunct inherits its head's passive auxiliary. Report all participles and clausal participles separately. | Tune further on UD; report all participles only | Prenominal participles ("a revised offer") never head an impact clause. The conj fix is a general grammar rule, not tuned to crash data. UD is a component check, separate from end-to-end metrics. |
| D-42 | Skip the PolNeAR quote-stripping check. Quote detection is evaluated through the `quoted` class of the text_source test set instead. | Download PolNeAR (github.com/networkdynamics/PolNeAR) and use it locally | The repo has no license and contains full article text from US publishers. Copying it would conflict with the project's copyright rule. The text_source test set measures quote detection on the target domain. |
| D-43 | Collection resilience: after 3 quick retries, the fetcher waits out a network outage (polls the host with waits of 1 to 10 min, up to 2 h) before failing a URL. Failures are recorded per URL and requeued with `collect.fetch <outlet> --retry-failed` (not blocks or 404s). Discovery records finished sitemaps, logs and skips failed ones, and treats a non-200 sitemap as failed rather than empty. | Fail fast and rerun everything | A Wi-Fi drop or sleep/wake would otherwise mark a run of URLs failed, and a 504 sitemap was being read as "0 URLs" and never retried (found on CTV). Running jobs keep their old code; a retry pass after collection covers them. |
| D-44 | Fetch all tier-2 slugs ("crash"/"collision" without a VRU word) even though CityNews tier 2 adds about 11 h at the 10 s crawl delay. | Sample tier-2 yield and stop early | Stopping early would bias the corpus toward stories whose slug names a pedestrian or cyclist. No deadline requires the shortcut. |
| D-45 | Uneven coverage (author's instruction, 2026-11-19). Outlet comparisons use only the window all outlets cover (latest first article to earliest last article; CTV starts 2025). Trends over time are reported within each outlet, never pooled. Pre/post Bill 212 comparisons use only outlets with articles on both sides. The write-up opens with a coverage table (in-scope articles per outlet per quarter). Pooled corpus-level rates (text_source, headline vs body, fatal) still use the full window and are labeled as pooled. | Compare outlets over the full window; pool trends | With CTV absent before 2025, full-window outlet comparisons and pooled trends would mix time effects with outlet composition. |
| D-46 | Wayback use (author's instruction, 2026-11-19). Toronto Police news releases come from Wayback Machine copies even though the TPS site blocks scripted access; blogTO and Toronto Today Wayback copies stay unused. | Use Wayback for all three; use it for none | Police releases are public government records, not commercial content, and the project publishes no release text beyond short excerpts. blogTO and Toronto Today are commercial outlets whose live sites block scripts; using archive copies to reach their content would get around that choice. |
| D-47 | Bill 212 cutoff = royal assent, 2024-11-25 (S.O. 2024, c. 25; ola.org bill status page). Articles published before it are "pre". | Introduction date (October 2024) | Royal assent is when the Act, including the bike lane provisions, came into force. Stored in config (`analysis.bill212_cutoff`). Crash framing is v1; the comparison is descriptive, not causal. |
